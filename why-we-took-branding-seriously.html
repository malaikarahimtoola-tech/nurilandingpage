why we took branding seriously from day one

ok, as promised, it’s finally here: everything nuri branding.

BY NURI NOVEMBER 2026 CHRONICALLY ♡
everything branding ♡
TRUST IS VISUAL ✦

we know the tech matters. a lot of our time goes into building an app that actually works for women managing chronic conditions, with RD-backed recipes and guidance you can trust. but we also knew from the start that the product on its own wouldn’t be enough.

THE FOUNDER OF PLAYHEALTH PUT IT BETTER THAN WE COULD

“I saw products with cleaner branding or hundreds of thousands spent on marketing per month win, while comparable or better products, built by clinicians or scientists, could barely find a foothold.”

that’s the reality of building in health. it’s one of the most crowded and noisy spaces out there, and it’s full of influencer content built for engagement rather than accuracy (we talked about this in #2). being right isn’t enough. people have to trust you before they’ll listen to you, and most of the time that trust starts with how you look and how you sound.

so we knew we wanted to build a brand with nuri from the very beginning, not bolt one on later. and that meant being intentional about every piece of it.

where the foundation came from

before nuri, i spent years in brand strategy at Ogilvy, helping global brands work out who they were talking to and how to show up for them. i didn’t expect to use that so directly in a startup this early, but it became one of the most useful things i brought to the table.

the first question was the same one i’d ask on any client project: who is this actually for? for us, the answer was clear: women. specifically, women managing chronic conditions who are tired, overwhelmed, and have already been through every diet you can name. women who have been talked down to, handed generic advice, or made to feel like their condition is their fault.

that answer shaped everything after it.

what we learned (and what we’d tell anyone building in health)

1. trust is visual before it’s clinical.

your credentials matter, but people decide whether to give you a chance within seconds, usually before they ever see who’s behind the advice. if your brand doesn’t feel trustworthy, your expertise never gets a hearing.

2. know exactly who you’re talking to.

“everyone who wants to eat healthier” isn’t an audience. once we got specific about her (what she’s already tried, what she’s tired of hearing, what would make her feel seen), every decision got easier.

3. write it down, even when you’re tiny.

we built brand guidelines and brand boards that our designers and interns can reference, and set up templates so everything we put out (the app, our website, chronically, our socials) feels cohesive. it sounds like a big-company thing, but it’s actually more important when you’re small and lots of different hands are making content. it’s how a tiktok, a recipe card, and an app screen all end up feeling like the same nuri.

4. warmth is a strategy, not decoration.

being warm and approachable is a core part of who nuri is. managing a chronic condition is already clinical, isolating, and exhausting. our brand is meant to feel like the friend who happens to have an RD on speed dial, not another doctor’s office.

that last one is the one we keep coming back to. if nuri feels cold or intimidating, it’s just one more thing to overthink.

the goal ♡
we want opening the app to feel like a breath out.

p.s. if you’ve ever told us our posts “feel like nuri,” that’s the brand guidelines working, and it genuinely makes our day <3
